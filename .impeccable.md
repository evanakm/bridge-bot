## Design Context

### Users
Casual bridge learners, solo players, and developers validating bot/gameplay logic. They use the app to sit at any mix of North/East/South/West seats, let bots fill the remaining seats, play a complete contract bridge hand, and optionally inspect all cards during practice.

### Brand Personality
Calm, strategic, table-focused. The interface should feel like a clear bridge table for actual play: composed, legible, lightly tactile, and respectful of the user’s attention.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a refined card-table direction rather than a landing page: muted baize greens, warm ivory cards, crisp suit colors, compact controls, and a board layout that keeps the current turn, contract, trick, and visible hands easy to scan. Avoid generic dark neon dashboards, oversized hero sections, decorative gradients, and card-heavy marketing compositions.

### Design Principles
- Make the game board the first screen; configuration should be present but secondary.
- Keep every legal action obvious and every illegal action unavailable.
- Support learning through practice visibility without making normal play feel cluttered.
- Preserve bridge table orientation: North top, South bottom, West left, East right.
- Favor dense, stable controls over explanatory text so repeated play stays fast.
